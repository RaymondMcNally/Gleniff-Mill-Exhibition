Sun on a physical model

The heliodon.

A bench sun simulator for site models at Gleniff's latitude, 54.4° N. Put your model on the turntable, turn it to the sun's bearing and slide the light to the sun's height: the shadows on the model are the shadows on the site, for any date and time. It is laser-cut from 6 mm plywood and lit by one LED.

02 · Using it

Three settings

Read the sun's height and bearing for your date and time off the setting table in the build guide, or from Sun height now and Sun bearing on the Sun & climate page.

  1. Put the model on the turntable with its north over the N on the scale ring.

  2. Turn the turntable until the sun's bearing is at the red index on the tab facing the arc.

  3. Slide the LED along the arc to the sun's height and tighten the wing nuts. Dim the room.

At 1:200 a 5 mm pin is a 1 m post. Check its shadow against Shadow of a 1 m post on the Sun & climate page.

04 · What it gets right

And what it doesn't

The heliodon shows the shadows of whatever is on the model, at the right angle, for any day of the year. Know its limits before you trust it.

Good for

  • Shadow of the ground, walls and your pavilion on a 250 mm model at 1:200 or 1:500.
  • Comparing times of day and seasons quickly, with the model in your hands.
  • Photographing a sequence of shadows for your site analysis.

Watch out for

  • The LED is 1.1 m away, so its light spreads: shadows at the model's edges point about 6° off (9° at the corners). The centre is exact.
  • The arc reaches 64° and the carriage can't go below about 1°. The highest sun at Gleniff is 59°.
  • Trees and the mountains aren't on the model unless you add them. A bigger model than 250 mm needs the projector instead.
