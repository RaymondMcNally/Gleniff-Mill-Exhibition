The kit, at the scale you choose

03 · Instructions

Build sequence

Read Reading contours first if contour plans are new to you. Then work in this order. The model is built as a hollow shell: each layer is a band that the next layer sits on, so it uses about a third of the board a solid stack would.

  1. Materials

    The materials list above gives the number of sheets, the baseboard size and the number of bags for the scale and board you chose. At 1:200 a 5 mm board is one metre of height.

  2. Laser cutting

    Each sheet has two operations. Run the blue engrave lines first as a light score, then the red cut lines. Don't move the sheet between them.

    Board thickness and laser power vary, so run a test square on an offcut before every new batch of board. 5 mm board may need two passes; check the cut has gone through before lifting the sheet.

    If the cutter can't manage 5 mm, choose 2 or 3 mm board above, or cut every sheet twice from 2.5 mm board and laminate the pairs. The heights stay true.
  3. Sort the pieces

    Every piece has its ID engraved where the next layer will hide it. Bag them by layer number as they come off the bed.

    14B2.1

    Layer 14 · tile B2 (row B from the south, column 2 from the west) · first of several pieces in that tile. Tile joints shift between odd and even layers so they never stack. Very small pieces may not have room for an ID: find them in the assembly guide.

  4. Baseboard

    Cut the baseboard (size above) and lay out the layer 01 pieces dry. Once the outline is square to the board, glue them down.

  5. Stack the layers

    Work upward one layer at a time. Each piece's outer edge sits on the blue engraved line of the layer below. Use the 3D assembly guide to find every piece for the layer you're on.

    • Thin PVA on the 15 mm overlap strip only; too much glue warps board.
    • Pin or weight each layer and let it set before you add the next.
    • Dry-fit a full layer before gluing any of it.
  6. Finish

    Check the outer edge: sand flush where tiles meet. Then the site is ready for the mill and your proposal.

    05 · Trees on the site

    Where the trees stand, and how big they are

    Half the site is under trees, and they shape everything: shade, shelter, sound, the views in and out. The drone lidar found 512 trees of 3 m and over. Each one is drawn here as a circle the size of its crown seen from above, shaded by height. Tap a tree to read its height and crown, and what that is on a 1:500 model.

    Show
    Template · 1:500 on A1 (PDF) Template · 1:500 on 4 × A3 (PDF) Tree schedule (PDF) Tree list (CSV) Tree positions · 1:500 (DXF)
    1. Print the template at 100% (actual size). The A1 sheet is the whole 1:500 model, 675 × 483 mm; the A3 tiles join on the lettered 50 mm squares.
    2. Lay it over the model, north to north, lining up the edges, the river and the mill.
    3. Prick through each tree centre with a pin. Or laser-etch the positions onto the base from the DXF (layer TREE_CENTRE).
    4. Make each tree to its crown circle and height: at 1:500, 1 m is 2 mm, so a 10 m tree is 20 mm tall. The schedule lists every tree.

    How it was made: tree height is the surface model (the top of everything) minus the bare-ground model, both from the October 2025 survey on the Lidar page. Heights are good to about ±1 m. Where crowns touch in the woodland, the split between trees is an estimate, and scrub under 3 m isn't shown. Three trees marked ? sit on the mill platform and may be part of the ruin.
